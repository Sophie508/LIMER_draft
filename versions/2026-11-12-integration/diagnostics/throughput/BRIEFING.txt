LIMER meeting briefing: system, contributions and results
Evidence date: 2026-11-12. This is an analysis of existing development runs.

1. SYSTEM AND DEMO
Suggested slide title: Detection and helper-based recovery
Show one logical sender and receiver using A and B. Each server has other
logical GPUs. After B degrades, A continues its work. Once suitable helpers
on the two servers finish their own current-phase work, the system probes
the three-leg relay route and coordinates its activation. B-prime then
rescues incomplete B chunks and may carry newly allocated chunks.
At each scheduler phase boundary, the binding resets to A/B eligibility.
This does not heal the fault or erase the received alarm.

Demo cue: show the first alarm, helper availability, activation, fresh data
on B-prime, and the final phase barrier. Use the same diagram throughout.
Any conceptual animation must say it is an illustration. An actual trace
replay must cite its recorded case. The old public demo describes an older
system and must not be presented as a recording of this integration.

Simple speaking line:
"We start with two active paths. If B becomes unhealthy, A keeps sending.
An available GPU on each server can form a relay path. After checking that
path, we use it to finish blocked transfers and send some new chunks."

2. CONTRIBUTIONS RELATIVE TO THE COLLABORATOR VERSION
Suggested slide title: What this integration adds
The collaborator version already had adaptive chunk allocation and GPU
proxy recovery. Do not claim either was first introduced by this revision.
This revision connects the service detector to the recovery loop, makes
route bindings explicit at each phase, and evaluates the choice to wait or
replace using observed progress and charged setup costs.
The benefit gate is an implemented research variant whose current
weak-loss result is worse than direct recovery. A lifecycle repair is an
engineering correction, not by itself a literature novelty claim.
Candidate research question: when should a degraded active path borrow
healthy helper capacity, and does that reduce the collective tail after
paying the cost of doing so?
No new literature novelty survey was performed for this briefing.

3. RESULTS THAT THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS
Suggested slide title: The recovery path works, but remains near A-only speed
Use completion_comparison.png or its editable SVG.
All five plotted rows have the same 16-rank, 30-phase, 15-GiB workload size.
Healthy A+B: 480.18 ms.
Blackhole A-only: 959.85 ms.
Blackhole direct recovery: 907.90 ms, 762 new B-prime chunks over 28 phases.
Weak-loss direct recovery: 694.96 ms.
Weak-loss benefit gate: 975.89 ms.
Healthy-normalized whole-job goodput is 480.18 / completion time. It is
52.9% for blackhole direct recovery, not a measured 52.9% NIC line rate.
The direct blackhole case is 5.4% faster than the all-rank A-only control.
That comparison does not isolate the proxy's causal benefit: unaffected
ranks still use both planes in the recovery arm.
The current related development suite has 34/34 timely fault-arm checks,
but shared inputs mean these are not a new independent 95% acceptance study.
Total job time and the <100-ms detection target measure different things.
No numerical tensor reduction or real GPU throughput is measured.

4. WHY HEALTHY PERFORMANCE HAS NOT RETURNED
Suggested slide title: Late helper availability and slow completion credit
Use phase_timing.png or its editable SVG.
In post-onset phases 3 through 29, healthy phases average 16.01 ms.
Direct blackhole phases average 31.53 ms. Helpers become available at
about 15.99 ms and the probe/report/coordination adds 5.18 ms, so the new
route activates around 21.17 ms into the phase.
Only 2.74% of new allocations from the two affected sources go to B-prime
in that window. A carries 96.48%; original B receives 0.78% exploration.
These shares describe fresh allocation, not rescued bytes.

For a fresh B-prime chunk, mean local-copy legs total 0.000623 ms, the
network leg takes 0.083225 ms, and the final source notification takes
1.008044 ms. Notification occupies about 92.3% of the full 1.091894-ms
route-to-logical-completion cycle. Four helper credits remain reserved
until that notification. Even ignoring data transit and contention, that
model caps a fixed helper route below about 2.08 Gb/s, despite its 10-Gb/s
network link. This is an analytical ceiling, not measured throughput.
The four-credit limit, control-channel latency and release-at-final-ACK
semantics are present in the collaborator source as well.

The benefit gate also has a concrete prediction failure. At time
118.828640 ms in phase 4, source 15 predicts that its remaining 15,072 B
bytes will finish in 0.016894 ms and chooses to wait. At 124.928640 ms,
6.1 ms later, exactly those 15,072 bytes remain. Its recent average ACK
rate overestimates progress of a stalled tail. This observation identifies
a model error; it does not attribute the entire gated slowdown to one cause.

NEXT DIAGNOSTIC CHANGES, NOT COMPLETED RESULTS
First separate physical resource release from logical completion, while
retaining explicit correctness tracking, bounded in-flight work and stale
ACK protection. Establish whether the 1-ms final notification is a
deliberate requirement or a conservative control-channel modeling choice.
Run any alternate model as a declared sensitivity condition, preserving
the original result. Do not set its cost to zero and call that a fix.
Then make the gate aware of per-chunk progress age and stalled-tail risk,
and evaluate the real phase completion time, including delayed retries.
Finally assess whether helper setup can overlap existing work while
respecting helper NIC/buffer capacity and the requested phase boundaries.
Helpers that are required to wait until a healthy phase is essentially
finished cannot reproduce healthy dual-plane timing from phase start.
An objective of nearly healthy steady service may require a different
helper-availability/scheduling design, not just a faster detector.
